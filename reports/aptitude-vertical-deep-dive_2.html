01

The demand is structural, not a fad

Psychometric screening is now a near-universal gate in graduate and professional hiring — used by over 80% of Fortune 500 and roughly 75% of the UK's Times Top 100, and by financial services, consulting, tech, law, civil service, and the military. Because it's the first filter after application, essentially every applicant to a competitive scheme has to sit one, whether or not they get hired. That's what makes the top-of-funnel audience so large.

Illustrative funnel for a single large graduate scheme. The prep audience is the top bar — not the bottom.

The trigger & the stakes

The buying moment is sharp: an email lands — "complete your SHL numerical & verbal assessment within 5 days" — for a format the candidate has often never seen. It's usually one attempt, with any retake gated behind a 6–12 month cooling-off. A weak score quietly ends the application.

Competitive schemes (Big Four, investment banking, top consulting) require roughly the 70th–80th percentile against a graduate norm group.

Why it drives prep (and anxiety)

Scores are norm-referenced, so candidates can't tell what "good enough" is or where the cut sits — which fuels practice demand and dread in equal measure.

Most see meaningful improvement after 5–10 hours over 1–2 weeks. That short, intense, one-off window is the whole demand curve — and, as §05 shows, the whole problem.

02

The search-demand map — and where it's winnable

The demand expresses itself as search, and it stratifies cleanly. The head terms are enormous but owned by incumbents with years of backlinks. The winnable demand is the long tail: candidates searching for the exact test or employer they're facing, where intent is highest and competition is thinnest — and where AI-cheap content lets you cover thousands of variants that no hand-authoring team can.

Where the demand concentrates

UK & EMEA is the epicentre — grad-scheme culture plus SHL's TalentCentral dominance. The whole prep ecosystem (JobTestPrep, AssessmentDay, Practice Aptitude Tests, GraduatesFirst) is UK-anchored.

India is the fastest-growing, via campus placement (Mercer Mettl, AMCAT) — massive volume, mobile-first, very price-sensitive. A credible start-market with global product reuse.

Grounding caveat — do this first

The stratification above is grounded in the demand structure (funnel math, incumbent user counts of 1M+ and 400k+, the query taxonomy). Exact monthly volumes need a keyword tool (Ahrefs / Semrush) — that pull, filtered by keyword-difficulty, is validation step #1.

Note also: queries asked inside AI assistants aren't in keyword tools at all, so real demand runs higher than they show — an answer-engine-optimisation opening.

05

The opportunity — and the ceiling to design around

The upside is clean: AI generation makes an infinite, auto-verifiable bank of numerical/logical items nearly free (you can machine-check every answer, which neutralises AI's usual accuracy risk), so you can out-cover and out-refresh the incumbents, give away a genuinely good free tier as the SEO wedge, and add the instant-feedback tutoring none of them have. That's how you get the "instant visibility" — rank the long tail, serve it at quality, convert the highest-intent moment in the funnel.

The honest ceiling LTV. Priya preps for two weeks, then disappears the moment she's hired. There's no habit, no recurring need — so a better product doesn't unlock proportionally more revenue. That single fact is why the 30-year incumbent still sells one-time packs and why VCs stayed away. Build the best aptitude tool in the world and you still hit this wall — unless the business model escapes it. Pick your escape route on day one, not year three.
1

Broaden the job-to-be-done

Go from "pass this one test" to a whole-job-hunt companion — CV, applications, aptitude, interview, assessment centre, offer — with value across the months-long, multi-application search. Extends the window within a hunt and earns a reason to return for the next one.

raises LTV · defends against a chatbot · scope creep risk
2

Geography-first grad funnel

Own one high-volume ecosystem end-to-end — UK grad schemes, or India campus placement — via university/society partnerships, employer-specific packs, and careers-service distribution. Density beats breadth; global product reuse stays intact.

distribution moat · keeps global upside · still per-candidate
3

Flip B2B / white-label

Sell to the demand side: license the prep to universities & bootcamps for their students (the free-access model — but you're the vendor), or to employers/assessment vendors for practice & onboarding. Institutional, recurring revenue that escapes the per-candidate trap entirely.

recurring revenue · breaks the ceiling · longer sales cycle
The durable moat Since AI-made questions aren't defensible, the one thing to build toward is a trusted, predictive score — "practise here, and your real result lands where we said." That concordance between practice performance and real outcomes is data a chatbot can't produce, and it's what converts free users to paid and employers to partners. Start collecting the signal from day one.
06

The v1 wedge — what to actually ship

Narrowest thing that proves the demand and the SEO thesis, built on the shared construct engine from the brief.

01

Free, ungated, high-quality practice acquisition · SEO wedge

The three core constructs — numerical, verbal, logical — auto-verified and timed, no signup. Point it at the long tail: pages for top publishers (SHL, CCAT, Korn Ferry, Aon) and named employers. Rank where incumbents are thin; serve better than the free swamp.

02

Instant AI explanation on every item the differentiator

"Why C, and why not A/B/D" on demand — the tutoring none of the incumbents have. Turns a static question bank into a coach, and it's cheap because the domain is self-contained.

03

Account + readiness estimate retention · data

Save progress, track weak constructs, show a percentile readiness read (the thing norm-scoring hides). Free — but now you have a returning user and the item-response data that calibrates the bank and seeds the concordance moat.

04

Paid depth monetization

Full-length timed mocks under real test-day conditions, employer- and publisher-specific packs, gamified-format practice, richer analytics. Priced for the deadline-driven moment.

05

Escape-route + moat build the actual company

Run the chosen escape route (§05) in parallel from early, and grow the practice-to-outcome concordance into a score users and institutions trust. This is what turns a good product into a durable business.

07

Risks & what to validate next

Primary

The LTV ceiling

Transactional, one-off use caps a pure prep business. Mitigation = pick an escape route (§05) before building, not after.

Watch

Moving target

Gamified / AI-scored formats keep evolving. Manageable — the constructs underneath are stable and prep follows fast — but it's a permanent upkeep cost.

Watch

Free-access headwind

Universities give the prime grad segment free incumbent access. Argues for the long tail, the B2B/white-label flip, or a start-market where this is weaker (India).

Watch

Head-term SEO is locked

Incumbents own the high-volume terms. The whole thesis rests on winning the long tail — which must be verified, not assumed.

Watch

Content QA

AI items ship subtle defects. Auto-verifiability makes this far easier here than elsewhere, but the generate → validate → calibrate loop still has to be built.

Watch

Validity tension

Test-makers actively try to make tests un-gameable, and regulators (EU AI Act, EEOC) scrutinise assessment. An adversarial-but-established dynamic, not a blocker.

  • Pull the numbers. Run the head / mid / long-tail keyword lists through Ahrefs or Semrush; rank by volume-to-difficulty. Confirms (or kills) the long-tail SEO thesis.
  • Pick the start-market. UK (highest intent, free-access headwind) vs India (highest volume, price-sensitive). Decide where density is winnable first.
  • Choose the escape route now. Broaden the job, geography-first, or B2B/white-label — the answer shapes the whole product.
  • Run a willingness-to-pay test. Does the deadline-driven, high-intent moment convert at a price that works against a low-LTV audience?
  • Design the QA + concordance loop. Item validation and the practice-to-outcome dataset are the real assets — architect them from the first build.
